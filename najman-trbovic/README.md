# Splitting of primes on modular curves in Lean

A partial Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of

> F. Najman, A. Trbović, *Splitting of primes in number fields generated by points on some modular curves*,
> Res. Number Theory **8** (2022), Article 28, [doi:10.1007/s40993-022-00325-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-022-00325-w),
> [arXiv:2009.02485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02485).

The Lean definitions start from the paper's explicit affine equations of the curves, not from the modular curves
themselves (see [Remaining formalization](#remaining-formalization)). Table 7 is formalized in its corrected form,
for non-exceptional points, as in the [erratum](https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~fnajman/errata_splitting.txt).
Some results about the cubic fields take a step of the paper's proof as a hypothesis; these hypotheses are named
below. No `sorry`; every result depends only on the standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
About 6,800 lines of Lean, on Lean v4.35.0 and Mathlib v4.35.0, using Lean's module system.

The Lean code was written by OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol at the author's request. The statements have not yet been
reviewed by the author, and there is no [Comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator) challenge for this
project yet.

## Results

- `table7_field_iff` proves the corrected non-exceptional scope of Table 7 for
  all 18 explicit models, including level 28. It checks all 253 listed
  prime–level pairs and produces a ramification witness for each of the 197
  omitted pairs. `quadraticOrder_isIntegralClosure` identifies the ring of
  integers, and `numberField_discriminant` proves the discriminant formula.
- `field_ramified_imp_square` proves the square-residue conclusion of
  Theorem 2.8 for the 13 pairs in Table 3. Lean checks the norm factorizations
  and integer Bézout certificates for primes greater than 100, and checks
  the small odd primes against the corrected Table 7. At 2 both residues
  are squares.
- `infinite_ramified_field_parameters` proves the affine-model arithmetic of
  Theorem 2.12 in each of the four families. It uses valuation-one lifts and
  the elementary argument behind Schur's theorem on prime divisors of
  polynomial values. An additional simple prime divisor distinguishes each
  new squareclass from any given finite set. `radicand_eq_of_isomorphic`
  proves that different squarefree radicands yield nonisomorphic fields.
- `Table2` proves every entry and footnote refinement of Theorem 2.2 for the
  explicit models. It uses prime-power congruences, integer norm identities,
  and quadratic reciprocity. `QuadraticSplitting` proves splitting and
  non-inertia in the actual ring of integers modulo the prime. `Errata`
  verifies an exceptional affine point at level 26 and counterexamples to
  the printed forms of Lemmas 2.6(b) and 2.9.
- `Cubic.descended_invariants` and `Cubic.descended_j` verify the corrected
  formulas for the explicit descended Weierstrass model. `Cubic.r_splits_mod_prime`
  proves the distinct cyclotomic factorization for every prime congruent to
  1 or −1 modulo 7. `Cubic.cubic_prime_splits_of_multiplicative_reduction`
  proves the splitting conclusion of Proposition 3.1 with the explicit
  cubic-fiber parametrization as a hypothesis. The proof passes from the
  projective Hensel lifts to a product over `ℚ_[p]` and then to a product of
  three copies of `ZMod p` in the quotient of the ring of integers. It gives
  a factorization of `(p)` into three distinct prime ideals of residue degree one.
  `Cubic.cubic_residue_splits_at_two` proves the latter conclusion for the
  explicit fibers at 2 without a reduction hypothesis.
  `Cubic.valuation_j_at_two` proves that the 2-adic valuation of the explicit
  model's `j` is a negative multiple of 14, as used in Proposition 3.4.
- `Cubic.cubic_torsion_group_has_marked_model` constructs the affine point
  `(u,v)` and a Weierstrass coordinate change from the actual
  `E(K)ₜₒᵣₛ ≃ ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/14ℤ` hypothesis. It uses a real embedding of the
  cubic field, an extremal two-torsion point, the affine addition formulas,
  and the inverse change of coordinates for the (q,t) chart.
  `Cubic.cubic_torsion_group_has_descended_j` proves that the two descended
  equations are elliptic and that both j-invariants equal that of the
  original curve after base change to `K`. The integer coefficient
  certificate for this identity is checked by the Lean kernel.
  `Cubic.cubic_prime_splits_of_torsion_and_rational_projection` combines these
  results with the ideal factorization once the rational projection is
  supplied. [Derickx–Sutherland's marked model](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/X1/X1_2_14.txt)
- `Cubic.cubic_prime_splits_or_full_rational_two_torsion` proves a dichotomy
  under the torsion and reduction hypotheses. Either all three nonzero
  two-torsion points are rational or every prime of multiplicative reduction
  splits completely. The proof lifts a simple root of the 2-division polynomial
  over the residue field, using the reciprocal chart at 2, and obtains three
  embeddings of the cubic field into `ℚ_[p]` when a two-torsion coordinate
  generates it. For normal `K`, `Cubic.torsion_group_has_galois_stable_order_seven`
  constructs the cyclic subgroup of order seven and proves its stability
  under the rational Galois action after base change.

## Remaining formalization

- Identify the 18 hyperelliptic models and their cuspidal loci with the
  corresponding modular curves `X₀(N)`. Extending Table 7 to the corrected
  all-points scope also requires the classification of exceptional points.
- Prove the rational projection step in Proposition 3.1. The marked point
  must have a coordinate in `ℚ` and another generating `K`. The published
  argument uses the rational points of the genus-four Jacobian and the
  image of the effective degree-three divisors in it. In Bruin–Najman’s
  proof, the rank-zero argument uses modular L-values and Kato’s theorem.
  [Bruin–Najman, §3](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa170323-20-9)
- The alternative proof
  `Cubic.cubic_prime_splits_of_torsion_and_integral_j_at_level_fourteen`
  reduces the splitting conclusion to the integrality of `j` for a rational
  elliptic curve with a rational two-torsion point and a Galois-stable subgroup
  of order seven. The two noncuspidal rational points on `X₀(14)` have
  `j = −3375` and `j = 16581375`. The level-14 integrality result is a
  hypothesis of the Lean theorem. [Mazur, Rational isogenies of prime degree](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~goldfeld/Mazur-Goldfeld1978.pdf)
- Verify the reduction-type assertions of Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 and the
  corrected geometric cusp statements independently of the splitting proof.

## Build and audit

With Elan installed, run these commands in the project directory.

```sh
lake exe cache get
lake build
lake env lean CheckAxioms.lean
```

The project pins Lean 4.35.0 and mathlib revision
`56a005855ea17e0b0958f73ec801ae140b0a1679`. The finite checks use
`decide +kernel`. The input coefficients and witnesses are in the Lean
files, and the build requires no external computer algebra software.

`AffinePointVariableChange.lean` is from Christopher Birkbeck's
[AINTLIB](https://github.com/CBirkbeck/AINTLIB) at revision `c3bc9bf`, unchanged except for its header,
which names the source. It provides the coordinate transformation of
affine points as an additive isomorphism. Its license is included as
`LICENSE-AINTLIB`.
