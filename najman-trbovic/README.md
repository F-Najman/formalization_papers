# Splitting of primes on modular curves in Lean

A partial Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of

> F. Najman, A. Trbović, *Splitting of primes in number fields generated by points on some modular curves*,
> Res. Number Theory **8** (2022), Article 28, [doi:10.1007/s40993-022-00325-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-022-00325-w),
> [arXiv:2009.02485](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02485).

* **Section 2, for the paper's explicit models y² = f_N(x) of Table 1** (not for the modular curves X₀(N) themselves)
  and the quadratic fields generated by their non-exceptional points: Theorem 2.2 (Table 2), Lemmas 2.4 and 2.6,
  the square-residue conclusion of Theorem 2.8 (Table 3), Theorem 2.12 (Table 4), and Theorem 2.13 with Table 7 in
  the corrected form of the authors' [erratum](https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~fnajman/errata_splitting.txt)
  (non-exceptional points; level 28 included).
* **Section 3:** the local and algebraic steps of Proposition 3.1, and the passage from E(K)_tors ≅ ℤ/2 × ℤ/14 to a
  point on the model of X₁(2,14). Proposition 3.1 itself is proved only *conditionally*, assuming the step of its
  proof that is not formalized (or, for normal K, the integrality of j on X₀(14)).
* **51 theorems and 1 definition, all proved.** No `sorry`. Every result depends only on the standard axioms
  `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.
* [`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean) states all of them, with `sorry` as proofs, together with every definition they
  use, over Mathlib alone (1,211 lines; the data of the 18 models and the tables make it long).
  [Comparator](https://github.com/leanprover/comparator) checks that the library proves exactly these statements
  about exactly these definitions.
* About 6,800 lines of Lean, on Lean v4.35.0 and Mathlib v4.35.0, using Lean's module system.

The Lean code was written by OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Sol (in Codex) at the author's request, using an AI review of the paper
(November 2026, not published); docstrings that mention "the review" refer to it, and are kept verbatim. The
Comparator challenge, the checks and the Palomar metadata were added afterwards, and two AI reviewer agents reviewed
the statements. The author has not yet reviewed the statements. See [`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml) for
the details.

**Where the formalization differs from the paper, and what it found.**

* `Level.table7` is the printed Table 7, entry for entry; what is corrected is the scope, as in the erratum: the
  statement is about non-exceptional points. An exceptional point at level 26 (over ℚ(√−11), in which 11
  ramifies) shows that the unrestricted statement fails (`exceptional26`).
* Lemma 2.6(b) and Lemma 2.9 are false as printed (`lemma26b_counterexample`, `lemma26b_conclusion_fails`,
  `lemma29_counterexample`, numerical facts whose link to the lemmas is explained in their docstrings); the
  formalization proves corrected versions where it needs them. The published erratum does not mention them.
* Theorem 2.8 at p = 2: "a is a square modulo 2" holds for every a, so the Lean statement says nothing there. The
  paper's footnote ("true with the exception of p = 2") suggests that a stronger condition was meant at 2; under the
  reading that a is a square in ℚ₂, the levels 26 (a = 13) and 50 (a = 5) are also exceptions: the point with x = 1
  lies over ℚ(i), in which 2 ramifies.
* For the elliptic curve E_u of Section 3, the Lean model `Cubic.descended u` has the paper's j-invariant, but its
  c₄ and Δ differ from the printed ones.
* Proposition 3.1: `Cubic.cubic_prime_splits_of_torsion_and_rational_projection` assumes the rational projection
  step (the marked point has one coordinate in ℚ and the other not); the paper proves this step with the rational
  points of a genus-four Jacobian (as in [Bruin–Najman, §3](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa170323-20-9)), which is not
  formalized. `Cubic.cubic_prime_splits_of_torsion_and_integral_j_at_level_fourteen` assumes instead, for normal K,
  that a rational curve with a rational 2-torsion point and a Galois-stable cyclic subgroup of order 7 has
  integral j (the noncuspidal rational points of X₀(14) have j = −3375 and 16581375); this is not a step of the
  paper's proof. Two variants, by a 2-torsion dichotomy, hold for every prime of multiplicative reduction, without
  the congruence condition of Proposition 3.1.
* Not formalized: the identification of the models and their cusps with X₀(N) and X₁(2,14), the classification of
  exceptional points, and the reduction-type assertions of Propositions 3.3 and 3.4 other than the 2-adic valuation
  of j.

## Definitions used by the statements

All of them are in [`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean), copied verbatim from the library:

* `Level` is the 18 levels N of Table 7; `Level.coefficients N` lists the coefficients of f_N (Table 1), constant term
  first, and `Level.form N m n` is the binary form n^d·f_N(m/n).
* `Represents N D`: D is squarefree, D ≠ 1, and D·s² = f_N(m, n) with gcd(m, n) = 1, n ≠ 0, s ≠ 0;
  `RationalTwistPoint N D`: the twist D·y² = f_N(x) has a rational point with y ≠ 0.
* `QuadraticField D` is ℚ(√D) as Mathlib's `QuadraticAlgebra ℚ a b` with ω² = a + bω, where (a, b) = ((D − 1)/4, 1)
  if D ≡ 1 (mod 4) and (D, 0) otherwise (`orderA`, `orderB`); `QuadraticOrder D` is the same over ℤ (the ring of
  integers). The instances `quadraticField` (a field, for squarefree D ≠ 1, given as `Fact`s) and
  `quadraticNumberField` are copied with their proofs.
* `FieldSpecialization N D`: there are x ∈ ℚ and y ∈ ℚ(√D) with y ∉ ℚ (`y.im ≠ 0`) and y² = f_N(x), i.e. a
  non-exceptional point generating ℚ(√D).
* `quadraticDiscriminant D` is D or 4D; `Unramified D p` is p ∤ quadraticDiscriminant D (used only in
  `eleven_ramifies_exceptional26`; the other statements use Mathlib's `Algebra.IsUnramifiedIn` for the ring of
  integers).
* `ResidueRing D p` is 𝒪/(p) for 𝒪 the ring of integers of ℚ(√D); `SplitsAt D p` is 𝒪/(p) ≅ ℤ/p × ℤ/p;
  `InertAt D p` is "𝒪/(p) is a domain"; `ReducedOrder D p` is (ℤ/p)[ω].
* `Level.table2Split`, `table2Unramified`, `table2NotInert`, `table2Odd`, `table2Real` are the columns of Table 2;
  `Level.table7` is Table 7.
* `NormCase` is the 13 pairs (N, a) of Table 3 (`NormCase.level`, `NormCase.radicand`); `InfinitudeCase` is the four
  pairs of Table 4; `OddSplitCase` is the eight odd-prime entries of the column "splits" of Table 2.
* `FiniteSquare p a`: a is a square modulo p.
* Section 3: `Cubic.r u w = u³ + u²w − 2uw² − w³`; `Cubic.fibre u w v z = r(u, w)·vz(v + z) + r(v, z)·uw(u + w)`, the
  curve (4) in bihomogeneous form; `Cubic.finiteChart u w` is the cubic in v of the fibre over (u : w);
  `Cubic.descended u` is the descended Weierstrass model E_u, with `g2`, `g6`, `g12`, `denominator`, `jDenominator`
  and the numerators `a2Numerator`, `a4Numerator`, `a6Numerator`; `Cubic.markedCurve u v` and
  `Cubic.normalizedMarkedCurve q t` are the marked models of X₁(2,14), with `markedSumTerm`, `markedDenominator`,
  `markedTNumerator`, `markedA2Numerator`, `markedA4Numerator`, `parameterNumerator`, `parameterDenominator`,
  `markedParameterPolynomial`; `projectiveDenominator`, `DistinctReductions` and `ThreeProjectiveLifts` describe
  three p-adic points with distinct reductions on a fibre.

## Building and checking

With [elan](https://github.com/leanprover/elan) installed, from this directory:

```sh
lake exe cache get                 # Mathlib's compiled files
lake build                         # the library
lake env lean CheckAxioms.lean     # prints the axioms of the 52 compared results
```

**Comparator.** [`comparator.json`](comparator.json) names 51 theorems (the 49 theorems of the table above and the two
theorems used inside an instance proof) and one definition, `residueRingEquiv`. Comparator builds
[`Challenge.lean`](Challenge.lean) and [`Solution.lean`](Solution.lean) (which imports the library), checks that each
compared declaration has the same type in both, that every other declaration they use (definitions, instances and
the proofs inside them included) is identical in both, that the library uses no axioms other than `propext`,
`Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and that the Lean kernel accepts the library (and, with `--paranoid`,
lean4lean, nanoda, con-leche and con-ron). In the pinned Lean version Comparator is part of Lake; it runs inside a
[bubblewrap](https://github.com/containers/bubblewrap) sandbox, so `bwrap` must be installed:

```sh
lake comparator --config comparator.json --paranoid
```

**Scripts** (Python 3, from this directory, after `lake build`):

* `python3 scripts/make_challenge.py [--check]` writes `Challenge.lean` and `comparator.json` from the library sources
  (or checks that they are up to date). Lean ([`scripts/challenge_deps.lean`](scripts/challenge_deps.lean)) lists the
  declarations that the statements use; the script copies them verbatim, each in the scope of its source file, and
  replaces each proof of a compared result by `sorry`. Since Lean shares an auxiliary proof between declarations of
  the same module, the Challenge empties that cache before the declarations of each library module, as a fresh
  module would, with a command `reset_aux_lemma_cache` defined at its top. Defining the command adds two auxiliary
  constants (outside the namespace `NajmanTrbovic`); running it adds no declaration.
* `python3 scripts/check_statements.py` checks that the 112 declarations the Challenge copies or states occur
  verbatim in the library as Codex wrote it (this repository's commit c95be25). Since then the library changed in
  one place, so that the Challenge and the library elaborate to the same constants: `TorsionToMarked.lean` imports
  `Mathlib.Algebra.Field.ZMod` publicly (without it, ℤ/2 in a statement there got its ring structure by a
  different instance path than in the Challenge).
